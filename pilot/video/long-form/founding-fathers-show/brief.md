# the fun founding fathers show

## premise

a sofa, a host, her assistant, and four men who have missed two and a half
centuries. each episode catches them up on one era they were not present for and
asks what they make of it: the world wars, the cold war, 9/11 and after, the
internet, the telephone in everyone's pocket. they arrive with 1787 still warm.

half civic education, half comedy, and the seam is the point. the audience learns
what happened because someone has to explain it to men who genuinely do not know,
which is the oldest teaching device there is.

## the division of labour that makes it work

the host runs the room and carries the comedy. the assistant is the one who
tells them: she explains the era, names the source out loud as each claim lands,
keeps the timeline, and is quietly running out of patience by minute twenty. that
split is not decoration. it is what lets the funny voice and the accurate voice
live in one episode without either softening the other. the host can be delighted
by the mess because the assistant is the one being careful.

## photoreal, and what carries it

the likeness is photoreal on purpose. caricature would take the joke away, and
the joke is that four men who wrote a constitution are being told about the
internet on a sofa.

what keeps that from being a problem is not a lower resolution, it is the frame,
and the frame is cheap:

- a permanent chyron, on screen for the whole episode, saying it is not a real
  interview
- the platform's altered or synthetic content disclosure on every upload, which
  is a requirement and not a courtesy
- the assistant never saying "this is what he would have said" without saying so
  first
- no photoreal likeness of a living person, ever, for any reason

nobody believes washington agreed to this. the show simply never pretends he did,
and every surface of the packaging says the same thing the chyron says.

## six voices, which is the actual engineering

this is the hard part of the show and the reason it is worth building in a system
that already has personas: six registers that must never blur.

| who | what they are for | register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the host | runs the room, owns the comedy | warm, fast, delighted by the mess |
| her assistant | tells them, sources everything, holds the timeline | precise, unhurried, out of patience |
| washington | the one the others defer to | formal, measured, appalled by informality |
| jefferson | the one who wants to write it down | expansive, idealistic, contradicts himself |
| hamilton | the one who asks what it cost and who paid | fast, combative, numbers first |
| franklin | the one who asks how it works | warm, ribald, interested in the plumbing |

each guest carries continuity across episodes: what he has already been told, what
he has refused to accept, the running joke he is in the middle of. by episode four
hamilton should still be asking what replaced the assumption plan, because he
asked in episode one. that is what "managing their personas separately" means in
practice, and the checker refuses two cast members who share a register.

franklin and adams swap cleanly if the room needs a different fifth century-old
grievance. it is one edit to the cast list.

## the accuracy rule, which is stricter than the comedy rule

a founder's position comes from something he actually wrote, and the assistant
names the source before the answer lands. where she is extrapolating, she says so
in the line before. no invented line is ever staged to sound like a documented
quotation.

two things this show has to get right rather than route around. the slaveholding
of the founding generation and the exclusions written into its documents are said
plainly when they matter, never softened into a joke and never parked on one bad
founder as if the others were innocent. and 9/11, the world wars and the civil
rights movement are explained, with the comedy coming from the guests'
incomprehension, never from the harm itself.

## the cut

every episode is repackaged into shortform. the reaction segment is what survives:
one founder's answer plus the host's face, 20 to 45 seconds, cut on the answer
rather than the setup. the chyron survives the cut with it, because a clip is
where a frame is most likely to be stripped, and the frame leaving first is
exactly how a joke becomes a fake.

## at episode three

the rhythm has settled into the correction as running gag: the guest who was right,
the guest who was wrong, the assistant's growing inability to keep six people on
one timeline. the civic segments are being clipped on their own and cited by
teachers, which is the success condition that matters.

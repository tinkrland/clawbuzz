# the founding fathers show

## premise

a sofa, a host, and four men who have missed two and a half centuries. each
episode catches them up on one era they were not present for and asks what they
make of it: the world wars, the cold war, 9/11 and after, the internet, the
telephone in everyone's pocket. they arrive with 1787 still warm and spend the
episode discovering how long it has been.

half civic education, half comedy, and the seam between them is the point. the
audience learns what happened because the host has to explain it to someone who
genuinely does not know, which is the oldest and best teaching device there is.

## why the likeness is fine and how it stays fine

nobody believes the show has George Washington. that is the first joke and it is
also the licence: the format is openly a comedy about history, the guests are
caricatures, and the audience is in on it from the title card.

two things keep it from drifting into something worse. the register is illustrated,
never photoreal synthetic footage of a real historical likeness that a stranger
scrolling past could mistake for archival material. and there is a permanent
chyron saying it is not a real interview, on screen the entire time, not a
disclaimer at the bottom of the description. the frame is not paperwork here; it
is the joke's footing.

## the accuracy rule, which is stricter than the comedy rule

a founder's position has to come from something he actually wrote, and the host
names the source before the answer lands. where the host is extrapolating, he says
so in the line before: this next bit is my guess at what he would have said. no
invented line is ever staged to sound like a documented quotation. every
historical claim in the catch-up segment is sourced in the notes.

the comedy lives in the gap between a man's assumptions and a century that
ignored them, so nothing has to be invented to be funny.

## handling the dark episodes

9/11, the world wars and the civil rights movement are the reason this show has to
be careful rather than the reason to avoid them. the host states plainly what
happened and to whom. the guests' incomprehension is the comedy. victims are never
the punchline, harm is never minimised for a laugh, and if a founder would have
said something ugly, the host does not rescue him, because the audience is
trusted to notice.

## at episode three

the show has settled into a rhythm where the correction is the running gag: the
guest who was right, the guest who was wrong, and the host's growing inability to
keep the timeline straight. the civic segment is being clipped on its own and
cited by teachers, which is the success condition that matters.

# stars sprangled banger

the folder name is spelled the way it was asked for. if the word should be
"spangled", it is one rename and one line in the checker.

## premise

one song, many genres. each episode rebuilds the same 1814 lyric and the same
public domain tune in exactly one genre, fully committed: pop, alt rock, gospel,
country, synthwave, shoegaze, orchestral. no medley, no ironic halfway house. the
genre is the whole idea, so a half-hearted arrangement has nothing left.

## why this works as a pilot and not a stunt

it is a production exercise with a fixed variable and a changing one: the melody
holds, everything else moves. that makes it a good test of the audio surface's
arrangement seam, because the seam has to describe instrumentation, tempo, key,
vocal delivery and one production rule that defines the genre, and a brief that
vague produces nothing worth hearing.

## the rights note, which is the part that is not a joke

the words are 1814 and the tune is older; both are public domain. that is what
makes this possible, and it is also why the note has to appear every episode: the
arrangement is new, it is not a recording of any existing performance, and no
copyrighted recording is sampled. the credit names the tradition being borrowed
rather than pretending the arrangement arrived from nowhere.

## the line that does not get crossed

the joke is the genre. the anthem is never the joke. no political endorsement, no
campaign adjacency, no parody of the people who served under it, and no imitation
of a living artist's voice as a shortcut to a genre.

## at episode three

two genres that should not work but do, and one that does not, kept in the feed
with a note about why it failed. a music pilot that only publishes its successes
is a playlist, not a show.

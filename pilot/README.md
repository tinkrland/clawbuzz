# pilots

a pilot is a show, not a format. each folder answers: who is the host, why does
this exist, what happens in the first episode, and what will we refuse to do even
if it would get a laugh.

## two files per pilot

`pilot.json` is the contract. the checker reads it, so the fields are the promise:
vertical, surface, format, cadence, the fiction or satire frame, the guardrails,
and episode zero as a list of beats. an edit here is an edit to what the show is.

`brief.md` is the reasoning. premise, why it is her and not someone else, the
structure of an episode, the frame, the guardrails in plain language, and what
success looks like at episode three rather than episode one.

## the vertical is not the surface

the folders name verticals, because that is how a person thinks about a show. the
surfaces are what the head actually has, so each pilot names both and the checker
refuses a disagreement:

| folder | surface | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `audio/podcast` | `audio` | episode plan, tts seam, chapters, transcript, rss |
| `audio/music` | `audio` | same engine, arrangement instead of narration |
| `video/long-form` | `longform` | written as video, chapters, 1080p targets |

## the frame rule

two of these three pilots would be irresponsible without their frame, and the
third needs a rights note. so the checker requires a fiction disclosure and at
least three guardrails, and refuses a pilot that lacks either.

- a fictional case presented as a true crime episode needs to say, out loud and
  every episode, that the case is invented.
- a comedy about the founders needs the chyron, because the chyron is the
  difference between a joke about history and a fake interview with a dead man.
